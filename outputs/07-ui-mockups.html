01

The day loop

Corner Vitals in practice. Four anchors, each tappable to expand. The centre of the screen belongs to the world and the action card — nothing permanent sits there.

02

Body and morale

The two systems most likely to read as the game cheating. Both get the same treatment: show the itemised cause, in plain language, at the moment it moves.

03

Doing things

Map, minigame, and the end-of-day compression card. The archery screen is the one that has to survive M1.

04

The contest

Intel is a resource. Rivals are never directly visible — the check-in is the only window, and it costs half a day.

The seasonal ramp does real work. It isn't decoration — it's a second, non-numeric channel telling the player how far into the run they are and how much trouble they're in. A screenshot from day 8 and one from day 52 are unmistakably different places. It also means the palette itself carries the decay thesis, which is the cheapest possible way to make a mobile survival game feel like it has a story arc.
05

Tokens

What a designer or an agent needs to build any new screen consistently. Colour values interpolate on a single t from 0 to 1 across the run, so there are only two palettes to maintain, not sixty.

Accessibility. Tier 1 from M2 means every corner vital carries a semantic label, focus order runs clockwise from top-left, and expanding a vital announces its full breakdown. The seasonal ramp must never be the only carrier of information — every state it signals is also stated in text somewhere on screen.

Contrast. Cedar's fog on mid sits near the edge of AA at small sizes. Body text uses snow, never fog, below 13px. Fog is for labels at 600 weight and above only.
