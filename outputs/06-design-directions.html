Five ways to
look cold

Three axes you can mix independently — palette, HUD layout, type. Pick one from each row and the phone below renders that combination with real day-34 game state. Nothing here is a finished screen; it's a way to argue about direction before anyone opens Figma.

01

Palette

All five are built from the reference: desaturated blue-slate, fog, snow, and a warm accent held in reserve. The variable is how much warmth is allowed in, and where it comes from.

02

HUD layout

The constraint that shapes all of these: a day is 3–7 slots, and the player needs calories, morale, temperature, cache and slot count legible at a glance without the screen becoming a cockpit. Each layout resolves that tension differently.

03

Type

Two roles minimum — a display face for headings and day markers, a utility face for numbers. Numbers matter unusually much here, so tabular figures are non-negotiable in whichever face carries the readouts.

04

Preview

Day 34, slot 2 of 4. Late lean season, morale falling, protein ceiling binding, cache down to three days. A deliberately uncomfortable moment to test a HUD against.

—
One idea worth stealing regardless of which direction wins: reserve saturated warm colour for fire and food alone. Everything else in the interface stays in the blue-grey range. Warmth becomes a resource the screen itself is short of, and a well-fed camp looks visibly different from a failing one before the player reads a single number. It costs nothing and it carries the thesis.

On the reference image. Colour palettes aren't protectable and drawing on that register is fine. The title lockup, the specific typographic treatment and the key-art composition are a different matter — those are trade dress on a currently-airing show. Worth keeping clear of when this reaches store assets, and worth flagging to whoever picks up action A1.

Fonts shown are Google Fonts stand-ins chosen for licence-free evaluation. Any of them can be swapped for a licensed equivalent once a direction is settled — the pairing logic is what's being tested here, not the specific families.
