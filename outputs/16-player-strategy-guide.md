# OFF THE GRID — Player Strategy Guide (Vancouver Island)

*Written against the current M0 build. Every figure is measured, not estimated. This is a designer's working document as much as a player's guide — where a choice is currently bad, it says so rather than pretending otherwise.*

> **Read this first.** All numbers below are for the **Vancouver Island** biome, and that biome has a strong character: it is a **fishing biome**. The salmon run is its defining feature. A guide for a boreal or Patagonian biome would rank these choices differently, and that is by design — see §5.

---

## 1. The biome you are dropped into

Expected gross calories per slot, before any of your choices:

| Season | Fishing | Trapping | Hunting | Foraging |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Salmon run (1–20) | **2,132** | 315 | 1,253 | 380 |
| Tapering (21–35) | 389 | 369 | **1,420** | 311 |
| Lean (36–50) | 117 | 482 | **1,079** | 147 |
| Winter (51+) | 38 | 427 | **803** | 108 |

**What this tells you as a player:**

- **The salmon run is the whole game.** Fishing pays 7× trapping for twenty days, then falls off a cliff to 1/38th of its peak by winter. What you bank in those three weeks decides your run.
- **Trapping is the only route that gets better as things get worse.** It rises from 315 to 482 while everything else collapses. It is your floor.
- **Hunting looks strongest on paper after day 20 and is not.** Gross calories are high, but a deer is 25 kg arriving at once, against a cache that holds ~38 kg and a body that can only process ~212 g of protein a day. Most of a big kill is lost unless you have preservation ready *before* you make it.
- **Foraging is never a primary route here.** It is a supplement — but a uniquely useful one, see §3.

## 2. Attributes

Measured value in extra days survived, per point, each on a plan that actually uses it.

| Attribute | Days/point | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Resolve** | **3.24** | The strongest pick by a wide margin |
| Cold Adaptation | 1.37 | Strong *if* you skimp on shelter gear |
| Bushcraft | 1.03 | Broad and reliable |
| Foraging | 0.57 | Niche, but see the carbohydrate note |
| Hunting | 0.35 | Weak here |
| Fitness | 0.24 | Weak unless you build around it |

### Resolve — the meta pick
Governs starting morale, how hard crises hit, and directly how likely you are to quit. Every other attribute helps you indirectly by improving food or warmth, which improves morale, which reduces quitting. Resolve skips that chain.

- **At 2–3:** you will very likely leave in the first fortnight. 36% of low-Resolve runs end by day 7.
- **At 5–6:** you survive settling-in and die of attrition around day 45.
- **At 8–10:** only 4% leave in the first week. This is the foundation of any deep run.

> **Designer note:** 3.24 days/point against Bushcraft's 1.03 is a 3× lead. This is still the meta and is flagged as unresolved in the diversity audit.

### Cold Adaptation — insurance, and only that
Reduces how much insulation the night demands. Worth **0.00** if your kit builds a good shelter, and 1.37 if it cannot. It only pays when something else has failed, which makes it a hard pick to justify deliberately.

### Bushcraft — the quiet generalist
Shelter build speed, cache capacity, comfort-project rate, and firewood yield per slot. It touches four systems, which is why it ranks second despite having no spectacular effect anywhere.

### Foraging — the ceiling-breaker
Low raw calories, but berries are the **only food in the game that is carbohydrate-dominant**, and carbohydrate bypasses the protein ceiling completely. When you are sitting on a lean cache and cannot absorb another gram of protein, berries are the only thing that still feeds you. Situational, and genuinely irreplaceable when the situation arrives.

### Hunting and Fitness — currently underpowered
Hunting moves big-game conversion from 7.6% to 12.4% across its whole range — a real relative gain on an irrelevant absolute base. Fitness drives prospecting, which is worth up to +10 days at Fitness 9, but only on a plan built around ranging out, and that plan underperforms simply fishing in this biome.

## 3. Archetypes

| Archetype | Strengths | Weaknesses | Best line of play |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Commercial Fisherman** 98 kg / 31% | Best in biome. Huge fat reserve, Cold Adaptation 8, and a gillnet in a salmon river | Slow, poor at ranging; badly exposed if the run is missed | Net the run hard, bank everything, coast the back half |
| **Ex-Military** 84 kg / 18% | Balanced kit, Fitness 8, no glaring hole | Master of nothing; middling reserve | Fish the run, trap the middle, hunt late |
| **Bushcraft Instructor** 88 kg / 26% | Bushcraft 8 — best shelter, biggest cache, most morale income | **Carries no bow.** Big game is permanently off the table | Build a cabin, run a big trap line, out-live everyone |
| **Hunter/Trapper** 92 kg / 24% | Hunting 8, best conversion on the one route that stays rich late | Wasted during the salmon run | Trap early, hunt the tapering and lean seasons |
| **Forager/Herbalist** 78 kg / 22% | Foraging 8, Resolve 6; the carbohydrate route sidesteps the protein wall | Thin reserve, low raw calories | Berries hard in the run window, trap through the lean |
| **Endurance Athlete** 72 kg / 12% | Fitness 9 makes prospecting cheap; best at finding good ground | **12% body fat is almost no battery.** Currently the weakest build in the game | Prospect the first ten days, then work the best ground you found |

> **Designer note:** the Endurance Athlete currently performs **worse than the fasting build** the morale system exists to defeat. That is a balance bug, not a difficulty setting, and is the top item in the diversity audit.

## 4. Suggested loadouts

Ten items. Some gates are absolute — no bow means no big game, ever; no tackle means no fishing at all.

**Universal core (7 items).** Fishing tackle or a gillnet · Axe · Knife · Pot · Sleeping bag · Tarp · Ferro rod

That leaves three to express your build:

| Archetype | The three | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial Fisherman | **Gillnet** (replaces line) · Snare wire · Paracord | The net is worth +4.6 days on its own. Skip the bow — Hunting 6 cannot convert anyway |
| Ex-Military | Bow · Snare wire · Saw | Only build that can genuinely run all four routes |
| Bushcraft Instructor | **Saw** · Snare wire · Paracord | Axe+saw unlocks the log shelter; the cabin is the whole plan |
| Hunter/Trapper | **Bow** · Snare wire · Saw | Bow is mandatory; Hunting 8 is the only sheet that converts big game acceptably |
| Forager/Herbalist | Snare wire · Paracord · Saw | Nothing gates foraging, so spend the slots on shelter and the trap line |
| Endurance Athlete | Snare wire · Bow · Paracord | Travel light; the plan is ground quality, not tools |

**Measured gear value** (days lost when dropped from a full kit): fishing tackle **−11.1**, axe **−3.8**, snare wire −1.1, sleeping bag −0.7, saw −0.4. Swapping line for a gillnet is **+4.6**.

**Never leave without:** tackle of some kind, and an axe. Those two are half of your survival.

## 5. Is this a fair test?

**No, and deliberately not.** Every number here is Vancouver Island, and that biome is fishing-dominant by design — the salmon run is what makes it that place.

This has two consequences worth being explicit about:

**For the player.** Your loadout should be chosen *knowing where you are going*. A bow is close to dead weight on Vancouver Island and would be the first item picked in a boreal biome. That is the right shape for the decision, and it is how it works on the show.

**For the designer.** Archetype balance currently cannot be assessed, because it has only ever been measured in one biome that happens to favour one route. The Hunter/Trapper and the Endurance Athlete may not be weak builds — they may be *correct builds in the wrong place*. Proving that needs a second biome with a different bias.

> **Open recommendation.** Biomes should keep their character rather than each being internally balanced — that is what makes biome selection meaningful and matches the source material. But archetype viability must then be measured **across the biome set**, not within one. Balancing every archetype inside a single biome would flatten exactly the variety that makes biomes worth shipping.

## 6. On lean animals, and how a catch should feel

A note on a design tension worth naming, because it is easy to get wrong in both directions.

The protein ceiling is real physiology and it is load-bearing for balance — "full cache, still starving" is the game's signature mechanic. But it produced a genuinely bad *feel*: a starving player snaring a rabbit received **zero** morale, because a hare is 0.8 kg and the reward threshold sat at 4 kg. Someone who has not eaten in three days is elated by a rabbit.

The fix was to separate the two questions. **Nutrition stays honest** — a lean animal really is lean, and a hare will not save you if you already hold more protein than you can process. **The moment of catching it does not depend on that.** Every catch now pays morale, scaled by size and multiplied by how desperate you were:

| Catch | Starving | Well stocked |
|---|---|---|
| Grouse | +5.5 | +2.2 |
| Snowshoe hare | +6.3 | +2.5 |
| Chinook salmon | +14.0 | +5.6 |
| Blacktail deer | +25.0 | +10.0 |

And the calories were never really the problem: on an empty larder a hare delivers **1,091 usable kcal** — a real day's food. It was only marginal when you were *already fed*, which is correct.

The general principle: **let the simulation be honest about the body, and let morale carry the drama.** They are different systems and they should answer different questions.
